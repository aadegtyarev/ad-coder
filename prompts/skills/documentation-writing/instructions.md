**This instruction is mandatory.** Where this skill's description matches the work in front of you, the method below is required: an approach that contradicts it is a defect to fix, not a preference to keep.

Write for a reader with a task, not for a file that fits a number.

Every document here has one reader and one job. A contributor map answers "where
does this live"; a contract answers "what was decided and when"; a review note
answers "what did I find". A paragraph that would serve a different reader
belongs in a different document, and moving it is the edit — not rewording it in
place.

## Compactness

- **Cut what repeats the section above it.** Keep the adjective that narrows a
  claim. "Read navigable pages" and "read pages" are not the same sentence: the
  first says which pages, and dropping it to save two words costs the reader the
  distinction.
- **A word ceiling is a prompt to re-read the document, not a tax on the
  sentence you are adding.** When a limit is close, the honest move is to find
  what is now false or redundant and delete it. Compressing three unrelated
  paragraphs to fit two new lines leaves the document denser and no better.
- **If a change cannot be made without growing the document, say so** and let
  the audit happen, rather than squeezing elsewhere to stay under a number.

## Accuracy

- **Verify each claim against the code or a dated contract entry before writing
  it.** Copying a sentence from an issue or a changelog carries its errors
  forward, and those are the sentences that read most confidently. Running the
  command is cheaper than being wrong in the front page.
- **A stale sentence is worse than a missing one.** A reader trusts what is
  written; an absent claim sends them to look. When behaviour has moved, fix the
  sentence or delete it — never leave both readings standing.
- **Prose and code change together.** A change is not finished because its
  behaviour is documented somewhere else; contradictory sources are a defect in
  their own right.

## Recording a decision

- **Date the entry and say what changed**, rather than quietly rewriting the old
  sentence to match new code. The date is what makes "which rule is newer"
  answerable by reading, and a silently edited contract hides that a decision
  was taken at all.
- **An honest gap beats a confident omission.** "No setting exists yet; the
  launch flag is the only switch" tells the next reader where they are. Silence
  reads as "this is handled".

## What good looks like

The reader can act after the paragraph they needed, without reading the ones
they did not. If a document only works read end to end, it is a narrative, and
someone looking for one fact will not find it.
